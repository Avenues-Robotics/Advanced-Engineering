## 1. Background and Mission Need
Northgate sells a basic gravity pet feeder: a hopper that refills a bowl as the pet eats. It is our best-selling pet product, but customer reviews and support calls point to three recurring problems:
- **Overeating:** Pets eat as much as they want. Owners of dogs and cats on a vet-recommended diet cannot use it.
- **Travel:** Owners away for a weekend cannot control when or how much their pet eats.
- **Tampering:** Determined pets knock the feeder over or pry the hopper open and eat several days of food at once.
**Mission need:** Pet owners need a way to give their pet measured portions of dry food on a schedule while they are away from home, without the pet being able to get extra food.
## 2. Scope
**In scope:**
- A countertop or floor unit that stores and dispenses dry pet food
- A way for the owner to set portion sizes and a feeding schedule
- Any mechanism, sensors, electronics, and firmware needed to meet the requirements
- A working prototype and a cost estimate for producing the product at volume
- A quick-start guide for customers
**Out of scope:**
- Wet or canned food
- A smartphone app (a button or dial interface on the unit is enough; see S1 for app connectivity as a should requirement)
- Packaging, retail branding, and marketing
- Safety certification testing, which Northgate will arrange after design selection

## 3. Concept of Operations
**Operating environment.** Indoors, on a kitchen floor or counter, at room temperature. Dry kibble from different brands varies in size from roughly 5 to 20 mm and in shape from round pellets to flat triangles. Homes may lose power for hours at a time.
**Users.** Owners are adults with no technical background who expect to set the product up from the box. Pets are cats and dogs up to about 35 kg, and some are persistent about getting to food.
**Scenarios.**
1. **Setup.** The owner unboxes the feeder, fills it with food, sets a portion size and schedule, and walks away. No tools, account, or internet connection are needed.
2. **Daily feeding.** At each scheduled time, the feeder dispenses a measured portion into the bowl. The pet eats from the bowl.
3. **Weekend away.** The owner leaves for 3 days. The feeder runs the schedule without anyone refilling or adjusting it.
4. **Power outage (edge case).** Household power goes out overnight. The feeder keeps its schedule and does not lose the settings.
5. **Jam (edge case).** A large or oddly shaped piece of kibble blocks the dispenser. The feeder detects that food was not delivered and alerts the owner.
6. **Tampering (edge case).** A large dog pushes, paws at, or tries to knock over the feeder to get more food.
7. **Cleaning.** Once a week, the owner removes the bowl and food-contact parts and washes them.

## 8. Evaluation Criteria
Proposals are evaluated in two steps.
**Step 1: Compliance.** Each proposal is checked against every shall requirement in Section 4 and every constraint in Section 5. A proposal must show a credible plan to meet all of them. Non-compliant proposals are returned without further evaluation.
**Step 2: Comparison.** Compliant proposals are compared in a decision matrix against **Northgate's current gravity feeder**, the product most of our customers own today. Each proposal is scored better (+), same (S), or worse (-) than the current feeder on each criterion.

## 9. Submission Instructions
**Format.** One PDF, 8 pages or fewer, plus appendices. Appendices may include CAD images, calculations, and test data.
**Required contents.**
1. **Summary:** one paragraph describing the proposed product.
2. **Concept description:** annotated sketches or CAD model of the proposed design and how it handles each ConOps scenario.
3. **Compliance matrix:** a table listing every requirement and constraint, how the design meets it.
4. **Risks:** the three largest technical risks and how the team will reduce each one before the Preliminary Design Review.
5. **Cost estimate:** a preliminary bill of materials showing prototype cost against C1 and volume cost against C2.
6. **Schedule:** a plan from selection to final delivery.
**Questions.** Submit questions in writing to the RFP point of contact by the end of Week 1. All questions and answers will be shared with every proposer. Any change to this RFP will be issued as a numbered amendment.
