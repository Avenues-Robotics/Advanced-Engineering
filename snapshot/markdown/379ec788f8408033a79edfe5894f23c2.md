# **FTC DECODE 2025–2026**
## **Shooter Subsystem Requirements**
### **Flowdown**
The following system-level requirements are owned by the shooter subsystem:
- The robot shall score artifacts into the classifier during the autonomous period.
- The robot shall score artifacts into the classifier during teleop.
- The robot shall score a minimum of 3 artifacts into the classifier per teleop cycle.
### **Operating Scenarios (ConOps)**
1. Close Shooting
	The shooter rapidly shoots three artifacts from the close zone and is then ready to receive more artifacts within 1 second.  There may be contact between robots from any direction; the robot shoots accurately while being pushed.
2. Far Shooting
	The shooter rapidly shoots three artifacts from the far zone and is then ready to receive more artifacts within 1 second.  There may be contact between robots from the side; the robot shoots accurately while being pushed sideways.
3. <span color="blue">Shoot while moving</span>
	<span color="blue">The robot roams around the close zone collecting artifacts and shooting them while on the move.</span>
4. <span color="blue">Shooter Jam (Edge Case)</span>
	<span color="blue">An artifact sticks in the shooter.  The shooter clears jammed artifacts automatically or with driver input.</span>
### **Functional requirements**
1. The shooter shall propel an artifact from the robot into the classifier opening.<br><span color="blue">Rationale: This is the primary scoring function of the subsystem. All high-value scoring (3 pts per artifact) flows through this requirement. It is a Key Driving Requirement.</span>
2. The shooter shall receive an artifact from the intake subsystem and hold it in a ready-to-fire position.<br><span color="blue">Rationale: Derived from the 3-artifact-per-cycle system requirement. Reliable staging is necessary to maintain cycle rate. If the shooter cannot consistently receive and hold artifacts, cycle rate is limited by handling failures rather than firing speed.</span>
3. The shooter shall return to a ready-to-receive state after each firing cycle without driver intervention.<br><span color="blue">Rationale: Driver attention during teleop must remain on navigation and strategy. Any manual reset between shots reduces cycle rate and increases driver cognitive load.</span>
4. The shooter **should** clear a jammed artifact and return to a ready-to-fire state.
	<span color="blue">Rationale: Derived from the Shooter Jam edge case scenario. Any jam without a recovery path results in complete scoring loss for the remainder of the match.</span>
### **Performance requirements**
**Shall Requirements**
1. Consistency: The shooter <span underline="true">shall</span> score a minimum of 25/30 balls from the far zone and 28/30 balls from the close zone in a testing scenario.
	<span color="blue">Rationale: A single-artifact reliability floor derived from the 3-artifact-per-cycle system requirement. Failing to meet this threshold means the cycle model breaks down and match scoring falls below target.</span>
	<span color="blue">MOP: Fire 10 sets of 3 artifacts from the following four set positions:</span>
		<span color="blue">Close (1 meter), pass = 28/30 artifacts classified</span>
		<span color="blue">Medium (2 meters), pass = 28/30 artifacts classified</span>
		<span color="blue">Oblique (1.5 meters at a 45 degree angle), pass = 28/30 artifacts classified</span>
		<span color="blue">Far zone, pass = 25/30 artifacts classified </span>
	<span color="green">MOP tolerance:  No tolerance on this value because a miss directly loses 3 points. Sample size of 10 trial sets justified by expected shot-to-shot variability in artifact trajectory and the high consequence of a false pass: a robot that passes at 5 trials but fails in a match costs too many points.</span>
	<span color="blue">TPM: Predicted goal 30/30 close zone and 27/30 far zone.   Track measured consecutive makes at each build iteration.</span>
2. Throughput: The shooter <span underline="true">shall</span> complete a firing cycle in 2 seconds or less from ready-to-fire position.
	<span color="blue">Rationale: A full teleop cycle (intake + transfer + fire + return) must complete in under 5 seconds to achieve 20 cycles per match. Transfer is estimated at 1 second, leaving 3 seconds for the shooter. This is a KDR — if cycle time exceeds 3 seconds, the match scoring model fails regardless of accuracy.</span>
	<span color="blue">MOP: Time from artifacts in ready position to third artifact leaving shooter as measured by video analysis. Pass = ≤ 2.0 seconds. Measured by video analysis over 10 consecutive trials.</span>
	<span color="green">MOP tolerance: Pass threshold = 2.0s ± 0.2s. Tolerance based on ±0.03 video frame rate, blurriness of balls exiting the shooter, and observed mechanical variability in early testing. Sample size of 10 trials justified by expected cycle-to-cycle variability in motor spinup time and the medium consequence of a false pass: cycle time is a KDR.</span>
	<span color="blue">TPM: Predicted value at project start: 3 seconds. Track measured average cycle time at each build iteration.</span>
3. Reliability: The shooter <span underline="true">shall</span> operate for the full 2.5-minute match duration without mechanical failure.
	<span color="blue">Rationale: Any mechanical failure during a match results in zero scoring for the remainder of the match. A 2.5-minute match at 20 cycles represents 60 firing events. Reliability over this duration is a KDR.</span>
	<span color="blue">MOP: Run 60 consecutive firing cycles without stoppage, jam, or component failure. Pass = 60/60 cycles completed.</span>
	<span color="green">MOP tolerance: Pass threshold = 60/60 ± 0. No tolerance — any mechanical failure during a match is a complete scoring loss. Sample size required: 3 full runs of 60 cycles, justified by the catastrophic consequence of a false pass and the difficulty of detecting intermittent fatigue failures at low trial counts.</span>
	<span color="blue">TPM: Predicted value at project start — 60/60. Track longest continuous run without failure at each build iteration.</span>
### **Interface requirements**
1. The shooter shall receive an artifact from the intake subsystem within ±10 mm of the shooter's intake point.
	<span color="blue">Rationale: Derived from the intake→shooter physical handoff described in functional requirement 2. Confirmed with intake subsystem owner. If the handoff position drifts beyond this tolerance, the shooter cannot reliably reach its ready-to-fire position.</span>
	<span color="green">Tolerance stack-up: Intake mounting tolerance ±3 mm, shooter mounting tolerance ±3 mm, chassis rail tolerance ±2 mm. Combined worst-case: ±8 mm, within the ±10 mm interface requirement but with limited margin. Verify early once both subsystems are built.</span>
2. The shooter shall signal ready-to-receive to the intake subsystem within 0.5 seconds of completing a firing cycle.
	<span color="blue">Rationale: Derived from functional requirement 3 (return to ready-to-receive without driver intervention) and the intake subsystem's need to know when it is safe to stage the next artifact. Confirmed with intake subsystem owner.</span>
	<span color="green">Tolerance stack-up: Signal latency budget of 0.5 s. </span>
		<span color="green">Mechanical reset time:  T_mech, measured/estimated from the shooter's return mechanism, this is the dominant term and must come from the actual design. </span>
		<span color="green">Sensor confirmation latency: ≤100 ms worst case, based on the REV Color Sensor V3's default 100 ms color measurement rate. </span>
		<span color="green">Control loop overhead: \~10-20 ms, one loop iteration to detect the sensor state change. </span>
	<span color="green">If mechanical reset tim plus 120 ms exceeds 0.5 s, either the requirement needs to loosen or the mechanism needs to reset faster.</span>
### **Should requirements**
1. The shooter <span underline="true">should</span> clear a jammed artifact and return to a ready-to-fire state within 10 seconds of jam detection, with or without driver input.
	<span color="blue">Rationale: Jams are a known failure mode for any mechanism that handles game pieces under load. A jam that requires match stoppage or robot shutdown results in complete scoring loss for the remainder of the match. Not a shall because jam frequency is expected to be low if the intake and transfer geometry are well-designed, and the added mechanical complexity of an active jam-clearing mechanism may not be justified if jams are rare in practice.</span>
	<span color="blue">MOP: Induce a jam by forcing an artifact into a blocked shooter state. Measure time from jam to ready-to-fire state. Pass = ≤10 seconds in 5 of 5 induced jam trials.</span>
	<span color="green">MOP tolerance: Pass threshold = 10s ± 1s. Tolerance based on ±0.5s timer reaction error plus observed variability in jam severity. Sample size of 5 trials justified by the difficulty of inducing consistent jams, jam conditions vary by artifact orientation and mechanism position, so a small sample with worst-case induced jams is more informative than a large sample of easy jams.</span>
	<span color="blue">TPM: Predicted value at project start: 5 seconds average clear time. Track measured clear time across jam types at each build iteration. If clear time exceeds 10 seconds consistently, evaluate whether active jam-clearing mechanism is warranted or whether the root cause is upstream in the intake/transfer geometry.</span>
2. The shooter <span underline="true">should</span> support independent adjustment of launch angle and/or launch speed by the control system, with a launch angle range of at least X degrees and a motor speed range of at least Y to Z RPM.  X, Y, and Z to be determined in initial testing.
	<span color="blue">Rationale: A fixed-angle, fixed-speed shooter can only score reliably from a single field position. A mechanically adjustable shooter allows the control team to implement variable-speed and variable-angle control, enabling scoring from multiple positions or while moving. Not a shall because the added mechanical complexity — additional actuators, pivot joints, and control interfaces — carries significant design risk, and a single optimized fixed configuration may achieve the target match score.</span>
	<span color="blue">MOP: Verify that the mechanism physically achieves the full specified angle range and speed range under load.  Angle and speed tested through telemetry.  Pass = full range achieved in 5 of 5 trials without binding or mechanical interference.</span>
	<span color="green">MOP tolerance: Pass threshold = specified range ± 2 degrees / ± 50 RPM. Tolerance based on expected mechanical slop in pivot joints and motor speed variability under load.</span>
	<span color="blue">TPM: Predicted range at project start — 30 degrees / ±500 RPM. Track achieved range under load at each build iteration. If range is insufficient, evaluate whether linkage geometry or actuator selection needs revision.</span>
### <span color="blue">**Key Driving Requirements**</span>
1. <span color="blue">Accuracy</span>
	<span color="blue">Highest design risk. Trajectory is sensitive to flywheel speed, artifact mass variability, and launch angle. A false pass in early testing where lab conditions are more controlled than match conditions is likely. Requires large sample size and worst-case condition testing.</span>
2. <span color="blue">Reliability</span>
	<span color="blue">Hardest to verify early. Fatigue, heat buildup, and game piece variability are difficult to replicate in short bench tests. Requires dedicated endurance testing late in the build cycle.</span>
