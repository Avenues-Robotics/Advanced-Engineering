This document outlines a set of engineering courses that fill a gap in Avenues’ formal STEM curriculum by giving students the opportunity to earn course credit through their participation in the existing FTC Robotics program, without changing the structure or culture of the current robotics program.
Students enrolled in these courses complete clearly defined engineering assignments and projects that align with real FTC competition goals while ensuring that they independently develop engineering knowledge and skills. The coursework is designed to formalize and document learning that already occurs when students fully engage in the FTC Robotics program, while adding academic rigor, reflection, and accountability appropriate for course credit.
Importantly, participation in robotics and enrollment in the courses are not mutually dependent:
- Students may participate in the FTC Robotics program without enrolling in a course.
- Students enrolled in a course work alongside teammates who are not enrolled.
- Course requirements focus on individual artifacts, even when projects are completed by a team, ensuring fair assessment regardless of team role or competition outcomes.
This model preserves the inclusive, collaborative nature of the robotics program while providing an academic pathway for students who wish to pursue engineering more deeply.
## **Instructional Model**
The courses are designed to operate alongside an after-school robotics schedule:
- Robotics meets 2–3 times per week for \~90 minutes, students enrolled in the course are required to attend 2 meetings per week from 4\:00-5\:30. This is equivalent to the five 72 minute meetings per 10-day cycle of a “long block” class.
- Course work is largely completed during robotics time, with limited at-home work (typically \<1 hour per week)
- Instruction is asynchronous and project-based, recognizing that team priorities shift throughout the season.
- Each course runs for a full academic year, structured around four approximately six-week terms
Students demonstrate learning through:
- design scopes
- code or CAD artifacts
- testing and data analysis reports
- reflection and synthesis reports
- engineering notebooks
- oral defense of design work
This mirrors authentic engineering practice and aligns well with project-based engineering programs at the college level.
