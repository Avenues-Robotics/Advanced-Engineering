### Overview
This is the first reflection you'll write for this course, due during during the first week of class. It has three jobs: it gives you space to think through why you're here and what you actually want out of this year, it gives me a sense of where you're starting from so I can support you well, and it gives you something to look back on at the end of the year to see how far you've come.
### Before You Start
- Find about an hour of quiet time somewhere you can actually think, not five minutes between classes and maybe even outside of the robotics space.
- Read through all the questions once before you start answering any of them. They connect to each other, and thinking about a later question often changes how you'd answer an earlier one.
- It's normal for reflective writing to jump around. If answering question 6 gives you a better idea for question 2, go back and change it.
- This first reflection isn’t graded, I just ask for your honest commitment to be honest and forthcoming.  I will give you some feedback using the rubric below as practice for future reflections.
### Part 1: Why You're Here
1. What made you want to take this course, beyond "I already do robotics"? What are you hoping to get out of it?
2. Name two or three long-term goals, things you want to be true in five or ten years, that this course connects to in some way. These can be about a career, a skill, or just the kind of person you want to become.
3. Name two or three specific goals for this course this year. Make them concrete enough that you could look back in June and honestly say whether you hit them.
### Part 2: What You're Bringing
1. Look at the Outcomes list for your course ([Mech](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18N3lLKN1lZ9QsHDgl5nFHsZMaP7xb76iGCQ0Ll37ZOA/edit?tab=t.0#bookmark=id.h1x50h9ti6ex), [SCS](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18N3lLKN1lZ9QsHDgl5nFHsZMaP7xb76iGCQ0Ll37ZOA/edit?tab=t.8v1e2qxy3s4o#bookmark=id.bmmbav5g1hj)). Where do you already have some experience, even informal experience from robotics itself, and where are you starting from zero?
2. What technical skills do you most want to build or get better at this year (CAD, code, a specific tool, a type of math, etc.)?
3. What "softer" skills do you want to build this year (time management, asking for help, communicating your ideas, working with a team, etc.)?
### Part 3: Looking Back
1. Think of something you accomplished recently, in school or otherwise, that you're genuinely proud of. What did you do that made it work, and what did you learn about yourself from it?
2. Think of a time recently you struggled or fell short of what you wanted. What happened, and what did you learn from it?
### Part 4: Making It Work
1. When and where are you actually going to do the work for this course? Be specific, a day of the week and a location is more useful than "whenever I have time."
2. Robotics has a way of eating every hour you give it. What's one thing you're going to do to protect time for the rest of your life this year, sleep, friends, other interests?

**Advanced**
Last spring I rebuilt our robot's intake mechanism three times before cities because it kept jamming on game pieces at odd angles. I finally fixed it two days before the competition by adding a second set of rollers at a different angle. I was really relieved and proud of myself for not giving up, even though there were a few nights I wanted to scrap the whole design. It reminded me of a coding project from freshman year where I kept debugging the same function until it finally worked, so I think persistence through repeated failure might be how I solve problems best. Looking back, I think the first two versions failed because I was fixing the symptom (jamming) instead of asking why it was jamming in the first place, once I diagnosed the angle issue the fix was obvious. This year I want to get better at diagnosing a problem before I start iterating on fixes, so I'm going to sketch out what I think is actually wrong before I touch CAD again.
