## Course Overview
Software and Control Systems 1 uses the control of robotic mechanisms to teach students the foundations of Java-based programming and control logic. SCS1 focuses on object-oriented programming, sensor integration, and feedback-based control through iterative implementation and reflection.
This course is structured around a series of projects that are intended to tie the FIRST Tech Challenge robotics season to professional software engineering practice. These projects include:
1. Requirements for a robot sub-system
2. Robot Control System Architecture
3. Autonomous Robot Drivetrain Implementation (Introduction to Java)
4. PID Control System
5. Robot Sub-system Software Implementation
6. Systematic Testing for Software Engineering
7. Post-competition Software Analysis
8. Term 4 Student-developed Project
In addition to their project work, students are expected to maintain an engineering notebook documenting their daily progress, decision making process, and reflections.
## Course Meeting Structure
This course and its sister course, Advanced Robotics Engineering: Mechanical, both meet outside of the daily 9\:00-3\:40 class schedule. There are no formal full-class meeting times. Instead, the course runs in a studio model in which students attend after school robotics sessions each week to work on individual assignments and collaborate with their teams. After school open lab sessions are intended for student and team work time, not direct instruction. Instruction in engineering course content will be available through a variety of text and video sources attached to each project.
Students will also meet one-on-one with Mr. Carpenter once per 10-day cycle for goal setting, feedback, and project support.
### Required Lab Time
- The Robotics Lab is open every Monday, Tuesday and Thursday from 3\:45-6\:00.
- Students are **required to accumulate 60 90-minute sessions** over the course of the year. This works out to approximately **two sessions per week**.
- Lab time is deliberately flexible to allow students to manage their after school responsibilities and robotics team work.
- Bi-weekly attendance reports will be emailed to students and families to help you track your attendance.
### Required One-on-One Meetings
- Students are required to attend a pre-scheduled one-on-one meeting with Mr. Carpenter once per 10-day cycle.
## Essential Questions
- How do engineers ensure a design is good before it's built?
- What makes an engineering decision different from a guess?
- How do engineered solutions evolve through iteration?
## Anchor Texts
The course will use a variety of topic-based reading passages adapted from professional and university-level engineering. In addition, the FIRST Tech Challenge Game Manual will provide the context for robot requirements.
## Homework Guidelines
The Advanced Robotics electives are designed to align with the FTC season and give students the flexibility to work on their team's schedule. Due dates for major projects and minor assignments will be set individually in collaboration with students. To ensure that students do not get too far off schedule, term-based deadlines for major projects will be enforced. For example, students will set their own due dates for Project 1, Project 2, and their associated minor assignments, but they must be completed during Term 1.
Most work for the Advanced Robotics electives is asynchronous, so in a sense all assignments are homework assignments. To protect homework time, students should plan to work on these assignments and projects during after school robotics sessions.
## Office Hours and Communication
Given the number of students in robotics, I will not be regularly available to meet one-on-one about coursework during after school robotics sessions. Students are strongly encouraged to set up a regular bi-weekly meeting during study hall or morning Z-block to review progress and get individualized feedback on their project work.

### Letter Grades
You will also receive a letter grade at each reporting period. Letter grades represent your performance at a particular moment in the year; earlier letter grades are not averaged and do not determine subsequent ones, including your end-of-year letter grade. Letter grades are based on your performance in the Evidence of Learning category, following the scale below, as well as on your record of timeliness in punctuality and submitting work.
A mark of "Not Yet" in either timeliness scale will result in a drop of your overall letter grade:
- For timeliness in submitting work, your grade will be lowered by one step (e.g., from B to B-).
- For timeliness in punctuality, your final grade will be lowered by one step (e.g., B to B-) for every six tardies. Your grade will not be lowered by more than three steps, or one full letter grade, for punctuality.
In this way, you will receive clear feedback on both your level of mastery of course goals and your record of timeliness for that semester.

## Academic Integrity
Academic integrity and honesty are the cornerstones of any educational institution, and so they are fundamental principles for our community at Avenues. While the topic of academic integrity is a broad one and no list can be entirely comprehensive, examples include plagiarism, cheating, and taking credit for work that is not one's own. Honesty, in all matters and on all levels, whether academic or otherwise, is essential to the trust upon which our community is founded.
Avenues recognizes the incredible potential of emerging technologies and artificial intelligence to enhance and enrich learning. However, it is essential we uphold academic integrity and our valued behaviors, fostering intellectual, creative, and critical thinking. Just as we credit human authors for their work, we need to also attribute text, insights, phrases, and other media formats generated by artificial intelligence. All sources, whether from human or artificial sources, should be properly cited and limited to a few sentences at a time to encourage original thought and learning. Additionally, please refer to each individual course syllabus for detailed information about specific acceptable AI guidelines and use cases.
